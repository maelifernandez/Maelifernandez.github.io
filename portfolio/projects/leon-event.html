Context

Léon Event is part of the Nanocristo group, which brings together several companies specialised in events and activities across Haute-Savoie, Switzerland and the Netherlands. With offices in Annecy, Geneva and Amsterdam, Léon designs tailor-made corporate events: seminars, parties and incentives, in France and abroad.

Client names and budgets are not published, out of respect for the agency's confidentiality.

My role

I support the agency's project managers in two ways, depending on the file. On some, I work in close coordination with the project manager, from building the concept to the final proposal. On others, I run the file almost independently, from the bid response to sending it to the client, with a light review before it goes out.

What I did

  • Translating client briefs into sourcing requirements.
  • Sourcing venues, hotels, activities and suppliers for each brief.
  • Building cost, capacity and logistics comparisons to guide the client's choice.
  • Producing proposal decks and integrating client feedback through to signature.
  • Negotiating rates with suppliers.
  • Organising site visits with suppliers to align teams and needs before the event.
  • Placing the orders needed to deliver the event.
  • Handling transport bookings, for individuals and groups.
  • Coordinating the event on the day.

On the ground

During my six-month internship, I coordinated one of the agency's events on site and took part in two team-building days for Mousqueton, another company in the group.

Geneva · 220 guests

A 90s-themed corporate evening, which I coordinated on site with my project manager.

Courchevel · around 150 participants

Team challenge built around skill-based activities. I set up the activity stations, then ran one station on my own (obstacle course, word puzzle, precision throwing) and managed the team rotations.

Les Arcs · 440 participants

Large-scale challenge with teams of 10 to 11 arriving in waves. Set-up with the activity team, then running a fire-lighting challenge while keeping each team on schedule.

Internal projects

Alongside client files, I also contribute to how the agency and its group work.

Ways of working

I helped make the agency more efficient by taking part in setting up new shared working methods.

Success story session for the group

A success story session is a presentation of concrete, successful examples, designed to share good practice between teams. With two colleagues, I organised one for all the companies in the group, on using digital and AI tools well: saving time, getting better organised and developing ideas.

Professional skills

Building a proposal deck

I learned to build a clear, convincing deck that the project manager can approve and send quickly: comparable options, explicit assumptions and nothing left to complete.

Managing large volumes of information

On large files, hundreds of hotels and suppliers are contacted. I strengthened my method for tracking every contact, quote and follow-up without letting anything slip.

Following up and negotiating

Replies rarely come at the first request. I plan follow-ups from the start, adapt to each country's working hours and habits, and negotiate rates to stay within the client's budget.

Preparing costed alternatives

For each key service, I prepare a costed and sourced alternative that can replace the first choice without delay.

Organising group travel

Departures from several cities, baggage rules, tight schedules: I learned to organise a group's travel so that everyone arrives in the right place at the right time.

Outcome

This internship confirmed my career choice. I ran complete files, sometimes almost independently: bid response, sourcing, negotiation, orders, then coordination on site.

I take three lessons from it. A good proposal always starts from the client's constraints. Organisation makes the difference as soon as a file involves hundreds of suppliers. And the event itself reflects the quality of the preparation. I want to keep growing in this direction and take on more responsibility.
