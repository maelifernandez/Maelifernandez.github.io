Context

Pour Faire Court is a short-film festival in its 9th edition, produced by students as part of the INSEEC master's programme. I co-led logistics for a one-night event welcoming an audience, guests and speakers on site.

My role

As logistics co-lead, I managed the venue, technical setup, suppliers and run of show together with my colleague. Of the festival's €5,000 budget, €3,500 went to logistics, which we managed as a pair: we approved every expense together as we went, so we always knew exactly what was left.

What I did

  • I wrote the event's logistics brief.
  • Using that brief, I identified suitable venues, contacted each one and handled the discussions, then presented a shortlist that the association voted on.
  • I followed the same process for suppliers, from decoration to the other services.
  • I co-managed the €3,500 logistics budget with my colleague.
  • I built the full run of show for the evening.
  • On the day, I co-led a team of 10 volunteers: briefing, role allocation and real-time problem solving.
  • We welcomed around 100 attendees, guests and speakers on site.

The hard parts

Part of the funding was withdrawn mid-project

The forecast budget was €10,000. Mid-project, with sourcing already under way, half of that amount was not allocated to us after all, leaving €5,000 for the whole festival. The team found new funding through four channels: an institutional negotiation I took part in, a donation drive, private contributions and a raffle, for which I approached local shops for prizes. I then adapted the choice of venue and suppliers to the real budget.

A private hire that wasn't private

The contract covered the auditorium, the cocktail room and the adjoining corridor, but the public kept circulating through our space on the night. I negotiated access restrictions with the venue on the spot. I now plan for door staff even when the brief says they aren't required.

The run of show had to be reworked live

A delay earlier in the evening pushed back the cocktail service. We reorganised the running order on the spot and finished on time. Since then, I always keep one team member without a fixed post, free to handle the unexpected.

Outcome

Pour Faire Court was my first experience of running logistics from start to finish, from the brief to the night itself. A one-night event leaves no second chance: everything depends on the preparation, and the run of show has to be precise enough for a whole team to follow.

The project also made me more versatile. Between finding funding, choosing suppliers, coordinating volunteers and handling surprises on site, I learned to switch roles quickly while keeping the shared goal in sight.
